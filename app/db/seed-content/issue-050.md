In the first half of 2026 the world shipped 461.3 GWh of lithium-ion energy-storage batteries, up 71% year on year. The headline is not the volume — it is where it went: for the first time, more than half of global ESS shipments went to markets outside China. China's share fell from 50.5% to 43.9%, North America grew 83%, and the Middle East and Australia pulled "other regions" up 119%. This issue reads the storage scoreboard the same way we read the EV one — shares, regional shifts, and the money flows that decide which factories get built next.[^1]

## Six makers, one Chinese top three

CATL kept the lead with 125.0 GWh and a 27.1% share, up from 25.6%. EVE shipped 48.0 GWh for 10.4%, and Hithium, the Chinese storage-cell maker, 46.2 GWh for 10.0%. The top three are all Chinese, and their combined share rose to 47.5% from 45.8%. Behind them the pack has compressed: CALB (31.5 GWh), Rept Battero (31.4 GWh) and Cornex (30.2 GWh) are separated by barely a gigawatt — three companies, six months, one GWh of daylight between them.[^1]

The Korean response deserves its own line. LG Energy Solution, the South Korean cell maker, grew 357% year on year — the fastest of any major supplier — to 12.0 GWh, with roughly 86% of that volume going to North America. Samsung SDI grew only 20%. Combined, the two Koreans hold 4.0%, against 47.5% for China's top three. This is not a parity story; it is a warning about where the margin is migrating — the two non-Chinese growth stories of the half are both playing the North American grid.[^2]

## Half of it now leaves China

The structural change is regional. China remains the single largest market at 202.5 GWh, up 49% — but its share of global shipments dropped 6.6 points to 43.9%. North America took 75.9 GWh (up 83%), Europe 72.7 GWh (up 74%), and other regions 110.1 GWh (up 119%, led by Middle East and Australia grid awards). Combined, non-China markets reached 258.7 GWh — 56% of the world, above half for the first time.[^1]

North America is where the shift bites hardest. CATL holds 38.8% there, but the share story is the rise of Hithium (17.2%, up 140%) and LGES (13.6%, from 4.2%). The American grid is no longer a CATL annex — it is a contested market where tariff walls and FEOC rules are reshaping supplier lists in real time. Read that against the EV side: in H1, non-China EV battery usage grew 26.3% while Chinese share of the global EV battery market still stood at 72.4%. Storage is the exception to China's dominance — and the reason is policy, not chemistry.[^2]

The flow is showing up in orders as well: in July CATL signed two European Na-ion storage agreements — 2 GWh with Solarpro, the Bulgarian renewables developer, and 5 GWh with Alfen, the Dutch grid-solutions company[^3][^4] — and in early August the Czech Republic's largest grid-scale battery, supplied by CATL-backed CNTE, came online.[^5]

## Grid is the anchor, homes are the surprise

By application, grid-scale storage remains the backbone: 347.0 GWh, 75.2% of the total, up 69%. CATL holds roughly 30% of it. The surprise is residential: shipments more than doubled to 47.7 GWh (up 128%), pushing the segment past 10% of the market for the first time. Rept Battero, the Chinese cell maker, leads there with 32%, EVE 25%, Great Power, the Chinese storage supplier, 23%. C&I storage grew a steadier 59% to 39.6 GWh, with CATL at 35% and Rept at 14%.[^1]

The residential growth is the tell. It is driven by recovering household demand in Europe and wider home-storage adoption in China — two markets where the customer is an individual, not a utility, and where supply chains are shorter. For Chinese suppliers, the profitable question of the next two years is not whether they can ship more grid GWh; it is whether they can follow the margin into residential, where brands and channels matter more than the PPA.[^1]

<!-- k:#5ADFC3|PART 04 · THE TAKE -->
## The take: the storage clock runs on policy

This page is journalism, not investment advice — we track the flow, you own the trade. But three signals from the storage half are worth holding on to. First, non-China markets are now the majority customer, and that majority is being assembled behind tariff walls — SNE notes supplier selection is shifting from price to supply-chain stability, local production and regulatory compliance. Second, the pack behind CATL is narrowing to a knife's edge, which means procurement negotiations in 2027 will be decided by capacity and compliance, not brand. Third, residential is the segment where Chinese suppliers are least entrenched and the rules are newest — that is where the next margin, and the next policy fight, will be.[^1]

None of these is a prediction. They are the coordinates. The storage market has stopped being a Chinese domestic story and started being a global one — and the rules that govern it are being written by the same governments that are writing the EV rules. Following the money, and the policy, is how you see the next map before it is drawn.
