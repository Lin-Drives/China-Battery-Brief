Three weeks ago the two sides were still arguing over principle, not numbers. On September 17 the EU asked China to cap its hybrid exports — a voluntary export restraint dressed up as a market-share ceiling — or face higher tariffs; Beijing rejected the demand as WTO-illegal the next day (No. 055).[^1] This week's deal is where that demand landed.

On Friday, EU trade commissioner Maroš Šefčovič said on X that the two sides had agreed to "halve HEV, PHEV exports" to the bloc. The post named no baseline, no timetable and no mechanism.[^1] By Monday, China's commerce ministry was describing the same arrangement as an "upward arrangement" on the 2025 baseline of 302,000 cars, one that "ensures room for Chinese companies to export, and stable growth."[^2][^3]

Both statements describe the deal reached in Beijing on October 8–9, at the second regular meeting of the China–EU trade and investment consultation mechanism, co-chaired by Šefčovič and commerce minister Wang Wentao.[^4] The two sides are not disputing the deal. They are disputing the denominator.

<!-- k:#2F6E77|PART 01 · THE WEEK -->
## A week of two numbers

On October 7, Bloomberg reported that the European Commission was preparing safeguard measures on Chinese hybrids, possibly tariff-rate quotas, as a test case for "trade rebalancing."[^5] Pre-meeting signalling began the same day: Yuyuan Tantian, a state-media commentary account, published "China–EU economic relations need a bigger consensus," noting that France and Germany had submitted a non-paper on October 5 demanding stronger trade-defence tools.[^6] The meeting ran October 8–9, and China's own statement on Friday disclosed no terms.[^1] It was Šefčovič's post that supplied the headline, adding improved EU companies' access to China's market and further eased rare-earth export licensing, and signing off as "a first step," with the two sides to reconvene by January.[^1]

On October 10, the European affairs department of China's commerce ministry published its own readout: the two sides had discussed electric-vehicle and hybrid trade "in depth, on the principles of respecting facts, non-discrimination and WTO compliance, avoiding escalation," and the EU would actively advance company price-undertaking procedures in the EV anti-subsidy case, along with related reviews.[^7] On Monday, October 12, the ministry's spokesperson took the question head-on and put numbers on the table. Citing Eurostat, the EU imported 107,000 hybrid cars from China in 2024 and 302,000 in 2025; the arrangement, the spokesperson said, is built "on the 2025 base, upward," guaranteeing export room and stable growth. Three principles run through it: respect the facts; non-discrimination, meaning China's quota will be no lower than any other source of imports; and WTO compliance, with China reserving every right to dispute settlement and trade compensation.[^2]

Both can be true at once. EU imports of hybrids from China rose from 3,800 cars in October 2024 to 50,000 in July 2026, the Financial Times reported; July's pace annualises to roughly 600,000.[^1] Half of that trajectory is almost exactly the 302,000 cars actually imported in 2025 — what the EU calls halving where things were heading, China calls growth from where things are. The option the two sides avoided was worse for the industry: the voluntary-export-restraint design that China's commerce ministry had already rejected on September 18 as WTO-non-compliant, and that Hualong Securities, a Chinese brokerage, estimates would have pushed Chinese market share below 15%.[^1][^8]

![Bar chart of EU hybrid-car imports from China — 107,000 in 2024, 302,000 in 2025 — with a dashed ceiling line above the 2025 bar marked as the negotiated upward arrangement; the EU's "halved" applies to the trajectory implied by a 50,000-car month in July 2026, not to actuals](/figures/058-two-baselines.svg "Figure 01 · One deal, two baselines: 107k, 302k, and a ceiling set above last year")

The clause worth watching is parity: China's quota will be no lower than any other origin's.[^2] That makes the ceiling apply to every supplier, with China guaranteed the best seat under it. Read together with the reserved WTO rights, the arrangement is less a concession than a template for managed trade — negotiated, non-discriminatory, and open to challenge if it fails.

The pressure behind the deal has not gone away. The Franco-German non-paper is still on the table, and China's commerce ministry answered it directly this week.[^9] At the G20 trade ministers' meeting in Milwaukee on September 30–October 1, talks on overcapacity, forced labour and most-favoured-nation treatment all ended without outcome documents.[^10] Global Trade Alert, a trade-policy monitoring outfit, chose this week to release a report on Chinese import penetration in five sensitive EU sectors.[^11] China also noted that it has faced 28 EU trade-remedy investigations since 2025, roughly half of them on chemicals.[^10] The deal narrows one front. It does not end the campaign.

<!-- k:#5A6B9E|PART 02 · THE EV TRACK -->
## Hybrids were the test; EVs are the file

Hybrids were never the main event for this industry. The electric-vehicle track is where the structural template gets set, and it moved too. Both sides recalled the January "soft landing" of the EU anti-subsidy case, under which the European Commission published general guidance on price undertakings — minimum-price commitments exporters can offer in place of the definitive countervailing duties imposed in October 2024, which range from 7.8% to 35.3% on top of the standard 10% tariff, or 45.3% combined at the top end.[^2][^1] The EU has now committed to actively advance company-level price-undertaking procedures and the related reviews.[^2][^7] If those go forward, the countervailing-duty differential stays with Chinese exporters as margin instead of crossing the border as tax; Hualong's reading is that pure-electric profitability stands to improve.[^8]

The calendar is filling up. A ministerial video call is scheduled for January 2027 and the mechanism's third regular meeting for March 2027.[^7] January is also when the US truce expires: the suspension of China's export controls on high-energy-density cells, graphite anodes and rare earths ends on January 10 (No. 057). The two negotiation tracks that have defined this autumn now converge on the same few weeks.

<!-- k:#6B6558|THE TAKE -->
## The take: a ceiling, not a wall

Set the dueling numbers aside and the deal has a clear shape. A unilateral curb was converted into a negotiated ceiling above current volumes, administered on non-discriminatory terms, with WTO recourse reserved. That is a better outcome than the quota regime sketched before the meeting, and it is also a deal the EU needs to sell at home as restraint. A number both sides can spin is a number both sides intend to keep.

The precedent matters more than the car count. Hybrids were the test case for "trade rebalancing," and the method — negotiate the ceiling, universalise the quota, reserve the litigation — is now available for every file behind it, starting with EV price undertakings. The battery industry spent the summer being regulated and the early autumn being negotiated about. It now has a mechanism, a baseline, and two dates in the first quarter of 2027.

<!-- k:#F0A832|ON THE HORIZON -->
## The Q3 silence

China's third-quarter reporting season opened on Monday: more than 80 listed companies had issued forecasts, five had filed full reports, and every A-share company must disclose by October 31.[^12] The battery chain's first numbers came from materials, not cells. Enjie, the Shenzhen-listed separator maker, forecast a swing to a net profit of 1.54–2.05 billion yuan for the first nine months, from a loss a year earlier — separator prices stabilising off their lows, utilisation high, richer mix. Its Q3 alone, at 720 million to 1.23 billion yuan, would be up 28–119% on Q2.[^13] Salt Lake, the Qinghai potash-and-lithium producer, guided profit up 88–92% to 8.84–9.05 billion yuan on steady potash, a ramped 40,000-tonne lithium carbonate line and firmer lithium prices; its Q3 alone would be down 8–14% from Q2.[^14]

The cell makers have said nothing. CATL, BYD, EVE Energy, Gotion and CALB have issued neither forecasts nor reports, and none is required to forecast. What is known going in: the first half was already the broadest profit recovery in years among listed Chinese storage and battery names, with second-tier players returning to profitability;[^15] September brought a round of storage-cell price increases led by CATL and EVE;[^16] and demand ended Q3 mixed. CAAM put September NEV sales at a year-to-date high as surging exports offset a domestic decline,[^17] while the CPCA counted retail down 16.7% but penetration at a record 67%, with BYD taking 31.1% of September exports.[^18][^19] Record volumes, shrinking retail, rising cell prices and a materials recovery already under way — and the cell makers' margins still the one thing the next two weeks will settle.
