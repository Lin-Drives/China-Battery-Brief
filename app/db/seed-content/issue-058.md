On Friday, the European side briefed that China had agreed to cut its hybrid-car exports to the EU by half. On Monday, China's commerce ministry described the same arrangement as an "upward arrangement" built on the 2025 baseline of 302,000 cars — one that "ensures room for Chinese companies to export, and stable growth."[^1][^5][^7] Both statements are about the deal struck on October 8–9 in Beijing, at the second regular meeting of the China–EU trade and investment consultation mechanism, co-chaired by commerce minister Wang Wentao and EU trade commissioner Maroš Šefčovič.[^2] The contradiction is not in the deal. It is in the denominators.

<!-- k:#2F6E77|PART 01 · THE WEEK -->
## A week of two numbers

The sequence matters. On October 7, Bloomberg reported that the EU was drafting a tariff-rate quota to curb Chinese hybrid imports, floated as a test case for "trade rebalancing."[^4] The same day, Beijing's pre-meeting signalling began: Yuyuan Tantian, a state-media commentary account, published "China–EU economic relations need a bigger consensus," noting that France and Germany had submitted a non-paper on October 5 demanding stronger trade-defence tools.[^8] The meeting ran October 8–9; by Friday the EU readout, carried by CnEVPost and CNBC, was that Chinese hybrid and plug-in hybrid exports to the EU "will be halved," with Šefčovič calling the talks a positive first step and improved EU access to China's market part of the package.[^5][^6]

On October 10, the European affairs department of China's commerce ministry published its own readout: the two sides had discussed electric-vehicle and hybrid trade "in depth, on the principles of respecting facts, non-discrimination and WTO compliance, avoiding escalation"; the EU would actively advance company price-undertaking procedures in the EV anti-subsidy case, along with related reviews.[^3] Then on Monday, October 13, the ministry's spokesperson took the question head-on and put numbers on the table. Citing Eurostat, the EU imported 107,000 hybrid cars from China in 2024 and 302,000 in 2025; the arrangement is built "on the 2025 base, upward," guaranteeing export room and stable growth. Three principles, the spokesperson said, run through the deal: respect the facts; non-discrimination — China's quota will be no lower than that of any other source of imports; and WTO compliance, with China reserving all rights to dispute settlement and trade compensation.[^1]

<!-- k:#B0432F|PART 02 · THE BASELINES -->
## Halved from what, growing from where

Both framings can be true at once, because each side picked the baseline that sounds better at home. The EU's "halved" is measured against what the bloc had on the table before the meeting: a quota regime designed to cut volumes from their trajectory — Hualong Securities, a Chinese brokerage, reads the outcome as rejecting an earlier voluntary-export-restraint idea that would have pushed Chinese market share below 15%, a design it says was set aside as WTO-non-compliant.[^12] China's "growth" is measured from the actual 2025 figure of 302,000 cars. A ceiling set above last year's actuals but below next year's unimpeded trajectory is a halving and an increase at the same time.

![Bar chart of EU hybrid-car imports from China — 107,000 in 2024, 302,000 in 2025 — with a dashed ceiling line above the 2025 bar marked as the negotiated upward arrangement; the EU's "halved" applies to the pre-meeting curb proposal, not to actuals](/figures/058-two-baselines.svg "Figure 01 · One deal, two baselines: 107k, 302k, and a ceiling set above last year")

The clause with the longest tail is parity: China's quota will be no lower than that of any other origin.[^1] That converts a bilateral curb into a most-favoured-quota — the ceiling applies to everyone, and China is guaranteed the best seat under it. Paired with the explicit reservation of WTO rights, it reads less like a concession than a template: administered trade, but on negotiated, non-discriminatory terms, with the option to litigate kept in the drawer.

The pressure that produced the deal has not dissipated. The Franco-German non-paper is still on the table, and China's commerce ministry answered it directly this week.[^9] At the G20 trade ministers' meeting in Milwaukee on September 30–October 1, talks on overcapacity, forced labour and most-favoured-nation treatment all ended without outcome documents.[^10] Global Trade Alert, a trade-policy monitoring outfit, chose this week to release a report on Chinese import penetration in five sensitive EU sectors.[^11] And China noted it has faced 28 EU trade-remedy investigations since 2025, roughly half of them on chemicals.[^10] The consensus narrows one front; it does not quiet the campaign.

<!-- k:#5A6B9E|PART 03 · THE EV TRACK -->
## Hybrids were the test; EVs are the file

For this industry, hybrids were never the main event. The electric-vehicle track is where the structural template gets set, and it moved too. The two sides recalled the January "soft landing" of the EU anti-subsidy case, under which the European Commission published general guidance on price undertakings — minimum-price commitments that exporters can offer in place of countervailing duties. The EU has now committed to actively advance company-level price-undertaking procedures and the related reviews.[^1][^3] If those proceed, the countervailing-duty differential stays with Chinese exporters as margin rather than crossing the border as tax — Hualong's reading is that pure-electric profitability stands to improve.[^12]

The calendar is now stacked. A ministerial video call is scheduled for January 2027, with the mechanism's third regular meeting set for March 2027.[^3] January is already the month the US truce — the suspension of China's export controls on high-energy-density cells, graphite anodes and rare earths — expires, on January 10 (No. 057). The two negotiation tracks that have defined this autumn now converge on the same weeks.

<!-- k:#6B6558|THE TAKE -->
## The take: a ceiling, not a wall

Read past the dueling numbers and the shape of the deal is clear: a unilateral curb was converted into a negotiated ceiling set above current volumes, administered on non-discriminatory terms, with WTO recourse reserved. That is a better outcome than the quota regime sketched before the meeting — and it is also, visibly, a deal the EU needed to sell as restraint. A number that both sides can spin is a number both sides intend to keep.

The precedent matters more than the car count. Hybrids were the test case for "trade rebalancing"; the method — negotiate the ceiling, universalise the quota, reserve the litigation — is now available for every file behind it, starting with EV price undertakings. The battery industry spent the summer being regulated and the early autumn being negotiated about. It now has a mechanism, a baseline, and two dates in the first quarter of 2027.

<!-- k:#7A6A3A|MARKETS -->
## The Q3 silence

China's third-quarter reporting season opened on Monday: more than 80 listed companies had issued forecasts, five had filed full reports, and every A-share company must disclose by October 31.[^13] The battery chain's first numbers came from materials, not cells. Enjie, the Shenzhen-listed separator maker, forecast a swing to a net profit of 1.54–2.05 billion yuan for the first nine months, from a loss a year earlier — separator prices stabilising off their lows, utilisation high, richer mix; its Q3 alone, at 720 million to 1.23 billion yuan, would be up 28–119% on Q2.[^14] Salt Lake, the Qinghai potash-and-lithium producer, guided profit up 88–92% to 8.84–9.05 billion yuan on steady potash, a ramped 40,000-tonne lithium carbonate line and firmer lithium prices — though its Q3 alone would be down 8–14% from Q2.[^15]

The cell makers have said nothing. CATL, BYD, EVE Energy, Gotion and CALB have issued neither forecasts nor reports — none is required to forecast. What is known going in: the first half was already the broadest profit recovery in years among listed Chinese storage and battery names, with second-tier players returning to profitability;[^16] September brought a round of storage-cell price increases led by CATL and EVE;[^17] and demand ended Q3 mixed — CAAM put September NEV sales at a year-to-date high as surging exports offset a domestic decline,[^18] while the CPCA counted retail down 16.7% but penetration at a record 67%, with BYD taking 31.1% of September exports.[^19][^20] Volume records with shrinking retail, rising cell prices, and materials already recovering: the cell makers' margins are the variable the next two weeks resolve.
