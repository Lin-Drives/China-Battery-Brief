Chinese storage suppliers booked over 3.5GWh of overseas business in the week of RE+ 2026. All three deals were structured as system supply, with controls, integration or service inside the package rather than cells alone[^1][^2][^3].

HyperStrong, a Beijing-based storage system integrator, won 1.6GWh of projects in California and Arizona, 400MWh of it built for data centres, plus a first 400MWh project in New York[^1]. It also took 100MWh in northern Canada and Alaska, and says cumulative awards in Texas and elsewhere now reach 4GWh[^1].

Canadian Solar's e-STORAGE arm signed with X-ELIO, a renewable developer, to supply five projects in Texas and New South Wales with 590MW/1.43GWh[^2]. The contract carries a 22-year long-term service agreement alongside the equipment, with commercial operation in 2027 and 2028[^2].

Sunwoda, a Shenzhen battery and storage maker, agreed a five-year framework with Acelerex, a US energy automation firm, to deploy 5GWh across utility-scale, commercial and microgrid sites[^3]. The first project contract was signed on November 17 at RE+ in Las Vegas, and the offer pairs a 261kWh liquid-cooled cabinet with Acelerex's control platform[^3].

## Three Orders, None of Them Cell Supply

| Deal | Capacity | What ships |
|---|---|---|
| HyperStrong, US and Canada | 1.6GWh, 0.4GWh and 0.1GWh | Battery systems; 400MWh sited at data centres |
| e-STORAGE for X-ELIO | 590MW/1.43GWh | SolBank systems and a 22-year service agreement |
| Sunwoda and Acelerex | 5GWh across five years | 261kWh cabinets with a microgrid control platform |

The three differ in size and market and share a delivery format. e-STORAGE supplies its own cells, inverters and plant controls as one package[^2]. Sunwoda pairs hardware with a third-party control layer[^3]. HyperStrong's data-centre capacity sits behind the meter, sized to a load rather than to a connection[^1]. In each case the cells are one component of what the buyer signs for.

HyperStrong says it has delivered over 70GWh across 400 projects and has over 2GWh under construction in Germany[^1]. Its order book now runs through 25 European countries and four African markets[^1]. What changed this week is the contract itself: equipment, controls and years of service priced together.

The longest contract in the three deals is the 22-year long-term service agreement e-STORAGE attached to its system package. It keeps operations, spares and performance with the integrator after the equipment is handed over, and the obligation follows the asset if the project changes hands. The buyer gets 1.43GWh of equipment and 22 years of cover; the supplier keeps 22 years of cost exposure[^2].

<!-- k:#2F6E77|PART 02 · THE BUYERS -->
## The Buyer Is Building a Data Centre

In a survey of 400 data-centre industry experts by Turner & Townsend, a project management firm, 56% named power as a major obstacle to delivery[^4]. Just over half said a utility connection takes one to two years. Almost a third said more than two[^4].

Construction costs for data centres rose 13% year on year in 2026, more than twice the global rate and about three times the ten-year average[^4]. Power costs $15.01 per watt in Silicon Valley and $10.51 per watt in Dallas[^4].

That gap between building time and connection time is where storage is being sold. Trina Storage, the storage arm of Trinasolar, launched a diesel-genset and battery hybrid aimed at AI data centres this month[^5]. FlexGen, a US integrator that began with off-grid microgrids in 2009, calls data-centre work a return to its first business[^6]. Lydian Energy brought a 150MW/600MWh system in Utah into commercial operation to support a Meta data centre[^7].

For a developer the calculation is a plant sized to the load and energised before the utility connection arrives. A data centre draws a flat load, so the battery covers the gap and displaces generator hours rather than trading daily spreads. The buyer is not a utility buying duration at auction; it is a builder buying time.

Chinese suppliers are entering that market at its edge. HyperStrong's 400MWh is a fifth of what it booked this week[^1]. Its importance is the standard it sets: capacity sited at the load, delivered as a system, and needed on the building's schedule rather than the grid's.

## The Cells Are Chinese, the Contract Is Local

Global shipments of lithium-ion storage cells reached 779.9GWh in the first nine months of 2026, up 73% from 451.5GWh a year earlier[^8]. The third quarter alone was 286.8GWh, up 58% year on year and 7% on the second quarter[^8].

Sixteen Chinese firms shipped 724.5GWh of that, 92.9% of the total, and took the top twelve places[^8]. CATL alone shipped 198.3GWh, up 75%, for 25.4% of the total[^8]. China itself took 313.1GWh; markets outside China took 466.8GWh, close to 60% of the total[^8].

Grid-side projects still dominate, at 575.9GWh or 73.8% of shipments[^8]. User-side storage, which includes the commercial sites that data centres resemble, grew fastest at 97.5GWh, up 152%[^8]. Commercial and industrial storage added 62.2GWh, up 57%[^8].

![Chart: 779.9GWh of storage-cell shipments split by demand region and by application, January-September 2026](/figures/064-shipment-split.svg "Figure 01 · Overseas is 60% of demand, the grid side 74%")

Customs data agrees: lithium-battery exports were worth $78.77bn in January to September, up 42.2%, on 4.3bn units, up 26.4%[^8]. The cell trade is not shrinking, and a system contract does not map onto a cell's customs line.

The unit of sale moved up: the contract is a 22-year obligation, not a delivery[^2]. That is why 3.5GWh matters less than the format. Planned cell capacity is close to 2TWh[^9]. Against that, a good week of orders is a rounding error. The scarce input is not cells. It is buyers willing to hand a supplier 22 years of obligation, and suppliers able to carry it.

<!-- k:#6B6558|THE TAKE -->
## The System Seller Wins the Order

A developer that waits two years for a grid connection is not shopping for the cheapest megawatt-hour. It is buying capacity that arrives before the grid does, and it pays for a supplier that can guarantee the system, the controls and the years after commissioning.

If the median service term in 2027 US data-centre storage tenders falls below ten years, the premium for systems and service goes. If owners bring system integration in house, these contracts revert to cell supply. Until then, the limit on Chinese cell capacity is not how many cells the lines can make. It is how many buyers can absorb a 22-year obligation.
