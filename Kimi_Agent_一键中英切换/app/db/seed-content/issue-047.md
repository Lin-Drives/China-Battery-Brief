CATL 宁德时代's Debrecen gigafactory enters the second half of 2026 with its entire 40 GWh first phase already sold out and cell production ramping after a staffing-driven delay. Down the road in Zaragoza, its €4bn Stellantis joint venture is rising. Add BYD 比亚迪's Szeged plant, Ford's licensed LFP lines in Michigan and a half-dozen second-tier projects, and the pattern is unmistakable: Europe's battery supply chain is being rebuilt by Chinese capital, on European soil, under European rules. This issue maps the new geography.

## Debrecen: sold out before it switched on

The numbers at Debrecen deserve a slow read. Total planned investment of roughly €7.34bn buys up to 100 GWh at full build-out — the largest Chinese greenfield commitment in Europe by a wide margin. Phase one's 40 GWh of annual capacity is, per Chinese state media, already fully booked by clients. Cell production was scheduled to start in early 2026 after Industry 4.0 equipment installation, with headcount passing 1,000 and targeted to reach 1,500 by Q1 2026.[^1]

Two details matter more than the headline. First, the delay: staffing shortages pushed production start from late 2025 into 2026. Hungary's labor market is tight, and CATL's local-hire approach — a deliberate contrast with earlier Chinese construction practice — means the ramp is hostage to regional workforce depth, not machinery. Second, the permitting: adjustments cut projected energy use by about 30% and potable water demand to roughly a third of earlier estimates, with carbon-neutral certification targeted within two years of launch. CATL is engineering the plant to pre-comply with the EU Battery Regulation's coming carbon-footprint regime. That is not corporate virtue; it is customer acquisition. The anchor clients are widely reported to be BMW (Neue Klasse) and Mercedes-Benz, though CATL has not officially named them.

## Zaragoza: the JV template, with caveats

If Debrecen is the wholly-owned flagship, the Stellantis joint venture in Figueruelas, near Zaragoza, is the politically palatable template. Announced in December 2024, the 50/50 entity — "Contemporary Star Energy" — carries a planned investment of €4.038bn (up to €4.1bn) for LFP cells and modules, with capacity of up to 50 GWh per year, phased and explicitly contingent on EU EV market conditions and government support.[^2]

Groundbreaking came on 26 November 2025. The construction model is telling: roughly 2,000 Chinese workers will support the build — a shift from CATL's local-hire approach at Debrecen — alongside 3,000 Spanish staff to be hired and trained. More than €300m in EU funding is secured. The 80-hectare site sits adjacent to Stellantis' Zaragoza plant, and Stellantis is the target customer for B/C-segment BEVs.

Watch the timeline gap. The official target is production by end-2026; construction completion has been reported as March 2028. Both can be true — phased lines can start while the site is unfinished — but the ambiguity is a reminder that "up to 50 GWh" is a ceiling, not a schedule. The phased structure gives both partners an off-ramp if European EV demand disappoints.

## The rest of the board

Debrecen and Zaragoza are the anchors of a much wider build-out:

| Player | Site | Investment | Capacity | Status (mid-2026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CATL | Erfurt, Germany | ~€1.8bn | 14 GWh | Operational |
| CATL | Debrecen, Hungary | ~€7.34bn (total) | 40 GWh phase 1 (100 GWh max) | Phase 1 fully booked; ramping 2026 |
| CATL/Stellantis | Zaragoza, Spain | €4.038bn | up to 50 GWh | Ground broken Nov 2025 |
| BYD | Szeged, Hungary | up to ~€4bn | up to 300k vehicles/yr | Trial production Jan 2026; series production Q2 2026 |
| EVE 亿纬锂能 | Debrecen, Hungary | ~€1bn | 28 GWh | Mass production targeted 2027 |
| Sunwoda 欣旺达 | Nyíregyháza, Hungary | ~€1.43bn | n/a | €264m EU-approved state aid, Aug 2025 |
| CALB 中创新航 | Sines, Portugal | ~€2.07bn | 15 GWh | Phase-1 construction launched May 2026 |
| Ford (CATL license) | Marshall, MI, USA | ~$3bn | 20 GWh | Production expected 2026 |

BYD's Szeged plant — its first European passenger-car factory — began trial production in late January 2026 and targets series production in Q2 2026, a few months behind the original end-2025 target, with the Dolphin Surf first off the line. EVE's Debrecen plant will make 46-series large cylindrical cells for BMW's premium program from 2027. CALB's Sines project, formalized with €350m in Portuguese incentives in January 2026, launched phase-1 construction in May with a €207.3m contract to Mota-Engil.

Two more pieces round out the board. Gotion 国轩高科's Göttingen base — which produced its first local battery back in September 2023 — began series manufacturing of 5 MWh BESS units with TÜV SÜD certification under the EU Battery Regulation in May 2026, and its GIB joint venture with InoBat in Slovakia has 20 GWh of trial production slated for this year. Spain is also spreading the money around: Gotion took ~€92m under the PERTE VEC program in June 2026 for cathode and black-mass recycling plants in Valladolid. And the cluster economics are compounding — Sunwoda's €264m-aided Nyíregyháza plant has already pulled in a €150m Boysen battery cover-plate factory next door.

And across the Atlantic, the licensing variant of the same strategy: Ford's wholly-owned Marshall, Michigan plant — scaled back from $3.5bn and 35 GWh to roughly $3bn and 20 GWh — will produce CATL-licensed LFP cells from 2026. Ford has since extended the license to stationary storage, launching "Ford Energy" in May 2026 with a 20 GWh/yr ESS plan built around CATL-licensed 512 Ah LFP cells. When direct investment is politically radioactive, royalty streams travel better.[^3]

## Why Hungary, and why now

The geography is not random. Hungary offers existing German OEM clusters, aggressive state aid (the EU approved €264m for Sunwoda's Nyíregyháza plant in August 2025), and a government that treats Chinese investment as industrial policy rather than a security question. Spain adds cheap renewable power and, in Zaragoza's case, a captive customer next door. Portugal is buying its way in with PIN status and €350m in incentives for CALB.

The demand side is equally concrete. Chinese brands hit a record 9.8% of the EU car market in April 2026 — more than 15% of the BEV segment — even with countervailing duties of 17.0% on BYD, 18.8% on Geely and 35.3% on SAIC stacked on the 10% MFN tariff. The January 2026 framework consensus on minimum-price undertakings signals that Brussels would rather manage Chinese competition than exclude it. Local cell production converts tariff exposure into employment, tax base and technology transfer. Every gigafactory is, among other things, a tariff-arbitrage instrument.

<!-- k:#8E97A8|THE TAKE -->
## The Take

**The uncomfortable truth for European policymakers is that the "de-risking" era is producing Chinese-owned localization, not European re-industrialization.** The capacity being built is real, the jobs are real, and the carbon-compliant engineering is real — but the process know-how, the equipment and the margin structure stay anchored in China. CATL's sold-out phase one tells you European OEMs have made their choice: they would rather buy cells from a Chinese plant in Hungary than from a European champion that does not exist. For investors, the trade is not whether Chinese incursion continues — that is settled — but who captures the second-order economics: the equipment suppliers, the materials JVs, and the licensing structures like Ford's that monetize Chinese IP inside protectionist walls. Europe wanted battery sovereignty. It is getting battery tenancy.
